## 4. What most detergents get wrong

- **Headline:** What most detergents get wrong.
- **Subhead:** Why we're making our own.
- **Instruction (desktop):** Pick a problem to see why it happens and the evidence behind it.
- **Instruction (mobile):** Tap a problem to see why it happens.
- **Desktop layout:** vertical tabs. The six problems are listed on the left, and the selected one shows an illustration, explanation, Proof box and "What we're doing" box on the right.
- **Mobile layout:** an accordion with the same content, using shorter copy where noted.

### 01 Too harsh

- **Explanation:** Detergents are often made strongly alkaline, because a high pH breaks down grease and stains fast. The same alkalinity can leave skin dry and irritated, and wears fibres down wash after wash.
  - Mobile: Detergents are often made strongly alkaline because a high pH breaks down grease fast. The same alkalinity can dry out skin and wear fibres down.
- **Proof:** Laundry powders typically wash at pH 10 or higher. Healthy skin sits below pH 5.
- **Source:** Skin pH: Lambers et al., Int. J. Cosmetic Science, 2006
- **What we're doing:** Each formula is being built to clean at the gentlest pH that does the job. We will print the number on the pack. (Mobile drops the second sentence.)
- **Illustration:** a pH scale from 0 to 14, marked "Healthy skin · below 5" and "Laundry powder · 10+".

### 02 Doesn't protect fabric

- **Explanation:** Detergents are sold on how clean clothes look after one wash. Very few are tested on what fifty washes do to colour, stretch and strength.
  - Mobile: Detergents are sold on how clean clothes look after one wash, not on what fifty washes do to colour, stretch and strength.
- **Proof:** A single 6 kg wash of acrylic clothes can shed over 700,000 fibres.
- **Source:** Napper & Thompson, Marine Pollution Bulletin, 2016
- **What we're doing:** Everyday Wash is being tested for fibre strength, fading and pilling across repeated washes.
- **Illustration:** a fraying yarn labelled "One 6 kg acrylic wash · 700,000+ fibres shed".

### 03 Not made for Indian conditions

- **Explanation:** Minerals in hard water latch onto cleaning agents before they reach the dirt, so you add more and still get stiff, dull clothes. Much of our laundry is also washed cold, straight from the tap.
  - Mobile: Minerals in hard water tie up cleaning agents, so you add more and still get stiff, dull clothes. Much of our laundry is also washed cold.
- **Proof:** India's drinking-water standard allows hardness up to 600 mg/L. Anything above 180 counts as very hard.
  - Mobile: India's standard allows hardness up to 600 mg/L. Above 180 is very hard.
- **Source:** BIS IS 10500:2012 · USGS hardness classes
- **What we're doing:** Every formula is tested at 200 to 300 mg/L hardness and 10 to 40°C water.
- **Illustration:** a hardness scale (soft, moderate, hard, very hard) from 0 to 600 mg/L. It marks "200 · BIS acceptable", "600 · BIS if no other source" and "Where we test: 200 to 300".

### 04 Lets the smell come back

- **Explanation:** Polyester holds on to skin oil, and the bacteria that feed on it produce the smell. A short wash rarely reaches that oil, so the odour returns the moment you sweat again.
  - Mobile: Polyester holds on to skin oil, and bacteria feeding on it cause the smell. A short wash rarely reaches that oil.
- **Proof:** After a workout, polyester shirts smelled significantly worse than cotton, with odour bacteria thriving on the polyester.
  - Mobile: After a workout, polyester shirts smelled significantly worse than cotton.
- **Source:** Callewaert et al., Applied and Environmental Microbiology, 2014
- **What we're doing:** Quikwash is being built for synthetics and tested for re-odour over repeated washes.
- **Illustration:** a polyester shirt ("stronger odour") next to a cotton shirt ("weaker").

### 05 Leaves stains behind

- **Explanation:** Blood, sweat and other intimate stains are proteins. They need enzymes to break down, and heat can set them, which is why a cold hand wash with bar soap so often leaves a mark.
  - Mobile: Blood, sweat and intimate stains are proteins. They need enzymes to break down, which bar soap does not have.
- **Proof:** Protein stains need enzymes to break down. Plain soap has none, so it relies on scrubbing alone.
- **Source:** How protein stains are removed
- **What we're doing:** Undergarment Wash is being tested on six intimate stains in a cold hand wash.
- **Illustration:** a bar of soap ("no enzymes") beside a protein stain being cut up by an enzyme.

### 06 Cuts corners to cut the price

- **Explanation:** Some powders are bulked out with ingredients that add weight but do no cleaning, so the pack looks like more for the money.
- **Proof:** Many detergent powders use sodium sulphate as a filler. It adds weight to the pack but does no cleaning.
- **Source:** Check the ingredient list on your pack
- **What we're doing:** Concentrated liquids at 25 ml a load, with a full ingredient list on every pack.
- **Illustration:** a pack cross-section split into "Cleaning ingredients" and "Filler · adds weight", labelled "Illustrative, not to scale".

## Open items before ads go live

1. **[Name]™:** the technology name has to come from the formulator, and check the trademark before using ™.
2. **Hero body copy:** it states results as facts ("provides advanced cleaning, precisely targets your stains and protects your fabric"). Under ASCI rules, either back each claim with test data or change it to "is designed to".
3. **pH promise:** "We will print the number on the pack" needs the formulator's target pH and a pack artwork commitment.
4. **Problem 05 source:** "How protein stains are removed" is not a citation. Replace it with a textbook or journal reference, or with your own test data once you have it.
5. **Hard-water testing:** "Every formula is tested at 200 to 300 mg/L and 10 to 40°C" is written as done. Keep it only once the test protocol is actually running.
6. **Placeholders:** [MONTH YEAR], [DATE], [contact email], ₹[X], the founder photo and the About us lines.
7. **Learn more links:** each card links to its product page (Everyday Wash, Quikwash, Undergarment Wash).
8. **Privacy and Terms pages:** needed before you collect any email addresses.
9. **Stock photos:** replace them with your own lab and home-trial photos as they happen.
